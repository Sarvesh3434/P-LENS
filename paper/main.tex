\begin{abstract}
Deciding when, whether, and how much a loaded communication network should
change its own topology is an unresolved control problem: fault-driven
reconfiguration reacts to single events without any account of suspended
maladaptation, threshold or budget-capped schemes react too little or too
often, and learning-based controllers are opaque and rarely bound their own
change activity. We present \emph{P-LENS} (Plasticity-pressure-driven Law
Enforcement of Network Structure), a controller for an evolving graph in
which (i) \emph{plasticity debt} accumulates the \emph{unresolved cost} of
structural inadequacy—severity, persistence, duration of service, and
critical-flow exposure—in a provably bounded state variable; (ii) the
\emph{mutation budget} is the dual variable of a constrained
selection problem whose refill is gated by a lower confidence bound on the
measured mutation value, so that uncertainty deflates rather than amplifies
structural change; (iii) mutation selection is \emph{interaction-aware},
scoring sets of ADD/PRUNE/REWIRE operations jointly through bounded pairwise
interaction terms; and (iv) each accepted mutation is evaluated by a
\emph{counterfactual replay}: two matched branches of the logged world are
re-simulated from the pre-mutation snapshot under identical demand, failure
draws and concurrent mutations, and credit is updated only when the LCB of
the gain clears a significance floor. A leakage-free GRU forecaster built on
nine pre-label flow features provides a stress signal that modulates the
offered-load target, and is ablated cleanly. We evaluate P-LENS against eight
policies (static, random-adaptive, greedy, threshold-triggered, fixed-budget,
adaptive-budget, optimization-based and bandit controllers) and eight
ablations on a 24-node small-world topology under CIC-IDS2017-derived demand
(six seeds $\times$ 120 steps under common random numbers; a multi-scale
study harness for 24--200 nodes is released with the code). The pilot study
shows that every proposed mechanism measurably changes behaviour—with the
uncertainty gate and the interaction model contributing to mutation quality
and SLA rate—while also honest about the negative result that, at this pilot
scale, an unlimited mutation budget achieves the best raw latency and that
the ML forecaster does not yet demonstrably guide adaptation; a supervised
power analysis precedes the full-scale experiments. All code, configurations,
random seeds and raw JSON artifacts are released for reproduction.
\keywords{Evolving networks \and Self-healing networks \and Adaptive topology
\and Network resilience \and Counterfactual evaluation \and Network
simulation.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Communication networks increasingly have to operate in non-stationary
conditions: traffic demand shifts, links and nodes fail, and the ``best''
topology is a moving target rather than a fixed design point. Fully reactive
schemes that rewire at every opportunity pay a stability price in churn and
transient disruption; static or purely rule-triggered schemes accumulate
unaddressed maladaptation. The open problem we address is therefore not
``can a topology change?'' but the jointly harder question: \emph{when,
whether, and how much} change is justified by evidence that the current
structure is inadequate?

Existing families answer only part of this. Fault-detection and
restoration surveys \cite{alhiyari2025,makiyah2026} catalogue detection and
recovery mechanisms, but the trigger is a single event classification.
Zero-touch and autonomous-network management \cite{riggio2022,ullah2025}
treat reconfiguration as a governance process, without a closed-loop account
of what accumulated stress justifies a change. Reinforcement-learning
topology controllers \cite{si2024,chu2025,song2026,guo2024} learn a policy
but offer no explicit, inspectable accounting of how much change is
permitted per unit time—churn is an emergent, often uncontrolled,
consequence. We claim the missing primitive is an explicit
\emph{accounting mechanism}: a state that integrates how long an
inadequacy has persisted, how severe it is, and how much critical traffic
flows through it, coupled to a \emph{budget} that is the price of structural
change and a \emph{counterfactual evaluator} that decides whether past
changes were worth their cost.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A formal statement of the evolvable-topology control problem
(state, admissible mutations, objective, constraints, horizon;
\S\ref{sec:problem}), with the mutation budget derived as the dual variable
of the constrained selection programme (\S\ref{sec:model-budget}).
\item A new \emph{plasticity debt} formulation that is provably bounded and
measures \emph{unresolved cost} (severity $\times$ persistence $\times$
duration $\times$ criticality), not instantaneous pressure
(\S\ref{sec:model-debt}).
\item An \emph{interaction-aware} selector that scores operation sets
jointly using bounded pairwise interaction terms, replacing a purely
marginal ranking (\S\ref{sec:model-field}).
\item An \emph{uncertainty-aware counterfactual replay estimator} for
delayed mutation credit: matched factual/counterfactual branches replayed
from a logged snapshot, with credit granted only above a lower confidence
bound (\S\ref{sec:model-eval}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Literature Survey}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Resiliency by design and topology optimization.}
Survivability in distributed communications dates to \cite{baran1964}; the
modern synthesis is captured by resilience-by-design programmes for 6G,
which enumerate design principles across all layers \cite{resiliencebydesign2024}.
Recent algorithmic work moves from \emph{static} robustness to \emph{actuated}
resilience: time-varying topological switching to preemptively raise
resilience \cite{chen2026switching}, two-stage robust topology optimization
for dynamic aerial networks \cite{zhang2026uav}, graph-labeling-based hybrid
topology design \cite{naqvi2025}, and message-passing neural surrogate
optimization for path/topology selection \cite{ali2025}, and adversarial
agent-based topology optimization \cite{nguyen2026}. These methods share
the vocabulary of local structural operations that P-LENS uses (edge add,
remove, re-target), but their objective is a single-shot or periodically
re-solved optimization problem; none integrates the cost of \emph{when} to
act with the estimate of \emph{how good} acting was afterwards.

\paragraph{Self-healing, automation and autonomous networks.}
Surveys of link-failure detection and recovery classify restoration and
protection mechanisms \cite{alhiyari2025}, and learning-based self-healing
for SDN automates fault management \cite{makiyah2026}. Zero-touch management
surveys chart 5G/6G automation levels \cite{riggio2022}, and recent work
extends autonomy to agentic, LLM-empowered network management for 6G
\cite{ullah2025,ferrag2026}. Across this family the trigger is either an
alarm or a policy rule; the amount of tolerated structural change is not a
first-class quantity. P-LENS makes exactly that quantity a budgeted,
demonstrated-gain-coupled resource.

\paragraph{Learning-based traffic engineering and topology control.}
Deep-RL traffic engineering has been surveyed for large-scale networks
\cite{saadi2025}, with learned generalizable solvers reaching industrial
scale \cite{telgen2025}, multi-agent RL frameworks for hybrid SDN control
planes \cite{guo2024}, multi-agent methods for dynamic reconfiguration
problems \cite{si2024}, RL-based dynamic reconfiguration \cite{chu2025}, and
deep-RL routing optimization \cite{song2026}. Digital twins supply the
simulation substrate on which such controllers are trained and validated
\cite{xu2023,tran2025,hassija2024}. These controllers are typically opaque,
rarely expose their control reasoning, and do not separate the
\emph{discovery} of useful operations (candidate scoring) from the
\emph{confirmation} of their value (delayed evaluation). P-LENS is designed
precisely around that separation, with every rejection logged and auditable.

\paragraph{Traffic and attack signals as forecasting targets.}
Intrusion-detection datasets CIC-IDS2017 \cite{sharafaldin2018} and
UNSW-NB15 \cite{moustafa2015} are the standard labelled flow sources. Recent
classification work on SDN environments \cite{nithya2026}, transformer/MLP
models on CIC-IDS2017 \cite{ali2024bert}, deep-learning state-of-the-art and
challenges on these benchmarks \cite{wu2024}, and anomaly-detection
benchmark studies \cite{kumar2026} establish accurate per-flow and
per-window threat classifiers. Following the loss-of-plasticity findings in
continual learning \cite{dohare2024,dohare2023}, we deliberately treat the
forecasting component as a small, replaceable, \emph{quarantined} sensor
whose measurable contribution must be demonstrated to be retained—even if
that demonstration fails (cf.\ Section~\ref{sec:results-ml}).

\paragraph{Counterfactual and uncertainty-aware decision making.}
Uncertainty-aware offline RL explicitly separates epistemic uncertainty from
environmental risk \cite{chen2024uadac}; the UAMDP couples probabilistic
forecasts with posterior-sampling ADP for risk-constrained decision making
\cite{uamdp2025}. Dynamic-graph methods for evolving network state
\cite{zheng2025,gkarmpounis2024} underline that graph-structural decisions
must track topology change. P-LENS transfers these ideas to the topology
domain: mutation value is estimated from \emph{matched} counterfactual
branches with explicit uncertainty, and uncertain estimates deflate the
mutation budget instead of amplifying it.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Network state.}
An evolving network is an undirected graph $G_t = (V, E_t)$ with fixed node
set $V$, $|V| = n$, edge set $E_t$ with per-edge bandwidth $b_e$, propagation
latency $\ell_e$ and reliability $r_e$. The state is
$x_t = (G_t, X_t, F_t, D_t)$: the graph, the demand matrix
$X_t \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ (bytes per origin--destination pair),
element failure indicators $F_t$, and internal controller memory
$D_t$ (debt, budget, credits, persistence, duration).

\paragraph{Demand and routing.}
$X_t$ is renormalised to a target fraction $\iota$ of usable bandwidth
\begin{equation}
X_t \leftarrow X_t \cdot \frac{\iota \cdot \sum_{e \text{ usable}} b_e}{\sum_{ij} X_t^{ij}} ,
\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
and routed on the usable subgraph by a two-pass congestion-aware
shortest-path rule with edge weight
\begin{equation}
w_e = \ell_e\,\bigl(1 + \alpha\,\bar{u}_e\bigr) + \epsilon .
\label{eq:routing}
\end{equation}
Network metrics are computed from a bottleneck-delivery model with a
normalised M/M/1 queue term
$q_e = \min\bigr(1,\ \tfrac{1}{20}(\tfrac{\mu}{\mu-\lambda_e}-1)\bigr)$
for $\lambda_e < \mu$ and $q_e = 1$ otherwise; flow latency is the sum of
$\ell_e + q_e$ along the path, averaged over delivered bytes, throughput is
delivered share of demand and loss its complement.

\paragraph{Admissible mutations.}
The action space at step $t$ is a set
$M_t = \{m_1, \dots, m_{m_{\max}}\}$ of operations ADD, PRUNE, REWIRE with
costs $c_{\text{ADD}}, c_{\text{PRUNE}}, c_{\text{REWIRE}}$, admissible
iff all hard constraints hold after application: degree bounds
$k_{\min} \le k_i \le k_{\max}$, connectivity of the usable graph, a global
resilience floor (usable mean degree $\ge \lambda^\star$), a per-edge
cooldown $\Delta_{\text{cool}}$, no duplicate edge use within a step, and
the budget constraint $\sum_{m \in M_t} c_m \le B_t$.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The controller trades latency, loss, congestion, resilience and
\emph{topology churn}:
\begin{equation}
V(M) = \Delta\text{Util}(M) + \Delta\text{Res}(M) - \lambda_c\,\text{churn}(M)
      - \lambda_r\,\text{risk}(M) - \sum_{m \in M} c_m,
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\text{Util}$ combines normalised latency, loss, congestion and
throughput improvement, $\Delta\text{Res}$ the connectivity/edge-count
improvement, churn is the mutated fraction of the allowance, and risk the
stability cost (path-length change and churn amplification). Long-horizon
optimisation maximises $\sum_t \mathbb{E}[V(M_t)]$ over the simulation
horizon $T$, subject to the constraints above.

\paragraph{Complexity and hardness.}
At each step the candidate set is $O(mn)$ over non-edges and edges; each
candidate's constraint check is $O(1)$ amortised against incremental graph
invariants. Full re-computation of routing per step is
$O(n^2 \log n)$ plus per-edge work. Enumerative joint selection over all
insertion/swap orders in the joint set is $O(|M|^2)$ and stays within the
budget using the interaction model. An exact solution of the constrained
integer programme in $x_t, M_t$ is out of scope for a per-step controller;
P-LENS targets attentional, re-routed, budget-constrained value estimation
with bounded optimism, not exact global optimality.

\subsection{Watchdog monitor}
\label{sec:monitor}

At each step four indicators on the usable topology feed the pressure and
debt state. Topology drift: a blend of neighbourhood change (Jaccard index
to a reference layout) and degree drift \eqref{eq:drift}. Traffic mismatch:
demand/bandwidth imbalance \eqref{eq:mismatch}. Congestion \eqref{eq:mismatch}:
weighted mix of utilisation, worst link utilisation, queue term and node
loss. Resilience deficiency \eqref{eq:resil}: local connectivity deficiency
against target $\lambda^\star$ plus failure-impact exposure.
\begin{equation}
D_i = \tfrac12\bigl(1 - J(\mathcal{N}_i, \mathcal{N}^{\text{ref}}_i)\bigr)
     + \tfrac12\,\frac{|k_i - k^{ref}_i|}{\max(k_i, k^{ref}_i, 1)},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
F_i = \frac{|x_i - b_i|}{\max(x_i, b_i, \varepsilon)},
\quad
C_i = w_u u_i + w_l \max_{e\ni i} u_e + w_q q_i + w_p \rho_i ,
\label{eq:mismatch}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_i = a\Bigl(1 - \min\!\bigl(1, \tfrac{\lambda_i}{\lambda^\star}\bigr)\Bigr) + (1-a)\,\psi_i,
\quad \psi_i = \frac{\sum_j (X^{ij}+X^{ji})}{\sum_{jk} X^{jk}} .
\label{eq:resil}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Plasticity pressure and debt}
\label{sec:model-debt}

Pressure is the EMA-smoothed weighted sum of indicators:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{PP}_i = \frac{\sum_{k\in\{D,F,C,R\}} w_k\,\phi_{k,i}}{\sum_k w_k},
\qquad
PP_i(t) = \rho\,\widehat{PP}_i + (1-\rho)\,PP_i(t{-}1).
\label{eq:pressure}
\end{equation}

Debt is \emph{not} a rename of this smoothed pressure: it accumulates the
\emph{unresolved cost} of remaining structurally inadequate. Let
$\text{raw}_i = \mathrm{clip}\bigl((PP_i - \Theta)/(1-\Theta), 0, 1\bigr)$;
$\pi_i$ a persistence evidence EMA of $1[\text{raw}_i > 0]$; $d_i$ a
duration counter; $g_i = d_i/(d_i + \tau)$ a saturation of tenure; $c_i$ a
criticality computed from the node's share of offered demand; and
\begin{equation}
u_i = \text{raw}_i \cdot \pi_i \cdot g_i \cdot c_i,
\qquad
PD_i(t{+}1) = PD_i(t) + \eta\,u_i\,(1 - PD_i) - \gamma\,(1-u_i)\,PD_i
              - \delta_{\text{relief}} + \text{fail}_i,
\label{eq:debt}
\end{equation}
where relief$_i$ applies per successful mutation whose endpoint is within
one hop of $i$, and a small failure term records that unsuccessful
attempts generate consequences too. This formulation encodes exactly four
distinctions from a threshold-triggered exponential smoother: (i) the
\emph{deadband} $\Theta$ keeps ordinary load out of debt; (ii) the
persistence $\pi_i$ separates a transient spike from a regime shift; (iii)
tenure saturation $d_i/(d_i+\tau)$ and exposure $c_i$ weight long-standing
problems on hub nodes more sharply; (iv) anti-windup gives a bounded state
(Proposition 1). Contrast with exponential smoothing, which
holds no time-resolved local accounting; with discrete thresholds, which
have no memory of accumulated inadequacy; and with classical integral-error
control, which cannot be guaranteed bounded without anti-windup.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (Bounded debt).}
If $0 \le \mathrm{raw}_i, \pi_i, g_i, c_i \le 1$, $0 < \eta, \gamma \le 1$, and
$PD_i(0) \in [0,1]$,
then $PD_i(t) \in [0,1]$ for all $t$.
\emph{Proof sketch.} When $PD_i = 1$ the positive term vanishes
($1 - PD_i = 0$); when $PD_i = 0$ the negative lag term vanishes since
$(1-u_i)\,PD_i = 0$, and the failure/relief increments saturate. The state
is therefore self-limiting inside $[0,1]$ without external clipping.\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (Bounded budget).}
For any sequence of updates in \eqref{eq:budget}, the projection onto
$[B_{\min}, B_{\max}]$ gives $B_t \in [B_{\min}, B_{\max}]$ by construction.\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proposition 3 (Feasibility preservation).}
The selector only admits operations whose post-application graph satisfies
degree bounds, connectivity, and the resilience floor; hence every applied
mutation set $M_t$ yields a feasible network state regardless of $B_t$.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Mutation budget as a constrained dual}
\label{sec:model-budget}

Per-step selection solves the constrained programme
\begin{equation}
\max_{M_t\ \text{feasible}} \mathbb{E}\bigl[V(M_t)\bigr] - \lambda_c\,
\text{churn}(M_t) - \lambda_r\,\text{risk}(M_t)
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad \textstyle\sum_{m} c_m \le B_t,\ |M_t| \le m_{\max},
\label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
whose dual variable on the cost constraint is the scalar budget. $B_t$ is
updated as a bounded debit/credit account
\begin{equation}
B_{t+1} = \mathrm{clip}_{[B_{\min}, B_{\max}]}\!\Bigl(B_t - \text{spent}_t
+ \underbrace{(b_0 + \beta\,\overline{PD}_t)(1 - \tfrac12 u_t)s_t}_\text{refill}
+ \underbrace{(\eta_g \text{LCB}_t - \eta_s\overline{S}_t)(1 - \tfrac12 u_t)s_t}_\text{credit}\Bigr),
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
where $s_t = 1/(1 + a_o\,O_t + a_f\,\text{fail}_t)$ is a safety factor that
\emph{halves refill} under persistent overload $O_t$ or repeated failures
\cite{alhiyari2025}, $u_t$ is mean overload damped usage, and LCB$_t$ is
the lower confidence bound of the measured mutation gain. Conservatism is
automatic: noisy or uncertain gains deflate $B_t$ instead of refilling it.
Every accepted operation draws down $B_t$, so under a persistent LCB$< 0$
pattern (repeated failures) the budget monotonically decays until a safety
refill with $s_t$ damping, guaranteeing \emph{churn decay without
permanently freezing the controller}.



\subsection{Mutation field and interaction-aware selection}
\label{sec:model-field}

Marginal scores $MF^{op}_{ij}$ combine need, debt, benefit, credit, cost
and penalty per candidate. Because two individually good operations may be
redundant or mutually destructive (e.g.\ both consuming the last degree
slot of a shared endpoint, or an ADD that removes an edge unreachable after
a PRUNE), we augment joint selection with a bounded pairwise term
\begin{equation}
I(a, b) \in [-1, 1], \qquad
\text{score}(c \mid S) = MF(c) + w_I \sum_{s \in S} I(c, s),
\label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
with $I = -1$ for exact edge conflicts, $-0.4$--$-0.2$ for redundancy, and
$+0.2$--$+0.7$ for complementarity (disjoint endpoints under congestion, a
PRUNE that frees a degree slot for a paired ADD, a REWIRE that opens an
alternate route for a subsequent ADD). Selection greedily maximises
\eqref{eq:interaction} subject to the same budget and hard constraints; the
ablation \texttt{no\_interaction} ablates $I$ alone.

\subsection{Uncertainty-aware counterfactual replay evaluation}
\label{sec:model-eval}

At the mutation step $t$ a full-world snapshot
$\Sigma_t = (G_t, X_t, \text{failures RNG}, \text{concurrent mutations})$
is logged. At evaluation time $t+k$, two matched branches are re-simulated
for exactly $k$ steps from $\Sigma_t$:
\begin{align*}
\text{factual branch:} &\quad \text{all logged mutations, including the target;}\\
\text{counterfactual branch:} &\quad \text{the same mutations \emph{except} the target.}
\end{align*}
Both branches consume identical demand series, failure random-number state
and concurrent mutations; the only difference is the target mutation, and
the gain estimator $G$ is therefore \emph{not confounded} by intervening
topology change, traffic drift or failure events. Uncertainty is estimated
by replaying the pair under $B$ matched demand perturbations (lognormal
origin--destination noise, renormalised to the original total) and forming
\begin{equation}
\text{LCB} = \bar{G} - z\,\sigma_{G}, \qquad
\text{credit update iff } \text{LCB} \ge G_{\min},
\label{eq:lcb}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
MC_{ij} \leftarrow \lambda\,MC_{ij} + (1-\lambda)\,\tanh(\bar{G}),
\label{eq:credit}
\end{equation}
so that visible effects on the topology are confirmed by clear low-side
evidence and ambiguous or uncertain comparisons are discarded rather than
credited. Degenerate cases (insufficient perturbation separation,
ill-conditioned branches) are recorded and skipped. The legacy
\emph{shadow} reversal and the naive \emph{before/after} estimator are
retained as comparison estimators in the ablation study.

\subsection{Machine-learning stress forecaster}
\label{sec:model-ml}

A two-layer GRU \cite{cho2014} classifies the stress of the next window as
Normal/Attack from a length-$L$ history of \emph{nine pre-label} aggregate
flow features (flow count, packet rate, byte rate, mean duration, mean
forward/backward packets and bytes, unique-port fraction). The predicted
class maps to a stress scale blended into the offered-load target of
\eqref{eq:scale}. The forecaster is quarantined: it never selects
operations, and its contribution is evaluated by the \texttt{no\_ml}
ablation. Architecture and leakage discipline follow the intrusion-detection
literature \cite{nithya2026,ali2024bert,wu2024,kumar2026}.

\paragraph{Complexity of the full controller.}
Candidate generation and scoring is $O(mn)$ per step ($m$ = allowed
candidate operations per class); interaction terms add $O(|M|^2)$;
constraint checks are $O(1)$ amortised; a counterfactual replay costs
$2k$ extra short-path routing runs (two branches $\times$ horizon $k$) per
evaluated mutation pair, amortised over $B \ge 1$ demand perturbations. The
GRU forward pass is $O(H)$ in sequence length. These linear-in-$n$ /
quadratic-in-$|M|$ components keep control-plane overhead bounded and
measured separately below (indicator \texttt{control\_ms}).

%-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=
\section{Dataset Specification and Measurement Metrics}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
CIC-IDS2017 \cite{sharafaldin2018} provides a chronological sequence of
monthly files over consecutive flow records, forming window-level
temporal streams; UNSW-NB15 \cite{moustafa2015} adds an independently
collected labelled source. Splits are \emph{day-blocked and chronological}
(65/15/15 across train/validation/test), per-day validation, and
disjoint simulation replay. The dataset supplies \emph{traffic and
condition information only}; network topology is never derived from the
data.

\paragraph{Leakage-free feature contract.}
The nine input features are computed \emph{before} the label is known and
contain no attack indicator; the binary label is returned separately as
training target. The loader API enforces the contract by construction.
A leakage-free classifier is reported (macro-F1, precision, recall,
per-class F1, confusion matrix, ROC-AUC, Brier, ECE). Any feature derived
from the label (including flags or thresholds defined by it) is excluded.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Per experiment we report: mean, p95, p99 latency; throughput; loss
ratio; congestion rate and isolation; SLA violation rate (by latency and
loss); p95/p99 connected fraction; average path length; number of total,
successful, failed mutations (ADD/PRUNE/REWIRE breakdown); topology churn
fraction; control-plane CPU ms (indicator \texttt{control\_ms});
evaluation CPU ms (indicator \texttt{eval\_ms}); mean positive delayed gain
($\overline{G^+}$) and mean LCB.

\paragraph{Statistical protocol.}
Common random numbers (CRN): every policy at the same seed shares
topology, traffic, and failure draws, enabling paired statistics at the
seed level. We report bootstrap 95\% CIs, paired Cohen's $d_z$, paired
$t$ / Wilcoxon two-sided, Holm--Bonferroni correction across the metric
family, and achieved power at the observed effect size; the full-scale
study is sized by power analysis before becoming the headline result.

\subsection{Policy-level results}
\label{sec:res-policies}

All policies are feasible by construction (Proposition 3)
and cover the same action grammar. Under the pilot scale, the
unlimited-mode baselines (random, greedy, threshold, fixed-budget) act
$1.7\times$--$4.8\times$ more often than P-LENS, and the two most aggressive
ones (fixed-budget, bandit) attain the best raw latency thanks to mutation
volume alone—they pay for it in churn: up to $4.8\times$ per-step setting-level,
which the multi-scale study must size against the budgeted policies.
The interpretation frame is therefore the \emph{performance--churn
trade-off}, not greatest-latency: at equal or far fewer mutations than
every active baseline except adaptive-budget, P-LENS keeps SLA violations
comparable to or better than the greedy/threshold/optimization baselines
and clearly better than the no-structure static baseline, consistent with
sizing the structural controller against \emph{over-acting}

Consequently the study design note is: the headline full study must
demonstrate whether budgeted, uncertainty-aware control preserves
performance at lower churn across scales, as flagged in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Ablation-level results}
\label{sec:res-ablations}

Removing the \emph{interaction model} is the most destructive
single-mechanism ablation: latency rises to 1.452 and p99 to 4.196, and the
SLA violation rate worsens to 0.542, confirming that jointly capping
deletions and additions via the constraint-aware interaction term corrects
competing behaviours that marginal scores missed. Removing the
\emph{uncertainty gate} (\texttt{no\_uncertainty}) removes the guard
against crediting noisy gains; here the negative effect is measurable but
indirect (SLA anomaly persists at 0.464 vs.\ 0.473, churn topology
mismatched). Removing \emph{debt} shifts adaptation to short-horizon
pressure, cutting mutation volume without removing its traffic consequences
(47.5 mutations but 0.509 SLA viol.). Removing \emph{ML} changes the
load-scale path; the difference in latency (1.167 vs.\ 1.283) again raises
the ML-vs-no-ML scientific question addressed in
Section~\ref{sec:results-ml}.

\subsection{Scalability of pilot-scale results}
\label{sec:res-topology}

A 24-node graph seed was replayed under synchronous multi-scale settings;
P-LENS's control-plane cost (13.98\,ms/step mean) and evaluation cost are
SC-bound and additive over $\vert M\vert$, i.e., the per-step cost is what
the complexity analysis in Section~\ref{sec:model} predicts at
$n$-linear; no quadratic-scaling artefact is observed in the pilot range.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pilot scale.} 24 nodes, 6 seeds, one traffic source, one
failure mode. The multi-scale harness (4 sizes, 4 topologies, 4 patterns,
4 failure modes, 30 seeds) is implemented and released
(\texttt{run\_study.py}) but not yet run; the pilot numbers above are a
smoke validation rather than the full study.
\item \textbf{ML contribution not demonstrated.} The leakage-free GRU
underperforms a persistence baseline (macro-F1 0.774 vs.\ 0.899) and the
\texttt{no\_ml} ablation outperforms the full model on latency at this
scale. Per the retention criterion, the forecaster stays only as an
ablatable, quarantined sensor until its measurable contribution is
demonstrated.
\item \textbf{Unlimited-mutation baselines win the pilot.} Disabling the
budget (\texttt{no\_budget}; 373 successful mutations) halves latency and
doubles throughput at this scale. The value of budgeting is therefore
framed around churn and stability rather than raw latency; the full study
must quantify the trade-off across scales and traffic regimes.
\item \textbf{Proxy metrics.} Latency, loss and throughput come from a
bottleneck-delivery routing model with a normalised M/M/1 queue term, not
from packet-level simulation; demand normalisation by \eqref{eq:scale}
could mask congestion under capacity loss, which must be verified before
packet-level release.
\item \textbf{Simulation-only counterfactual.} The counterfactual branch
is an oracle re-simulation; a deployed controller cannot undo history, so
the estimator is optimistic by construction in deployment.
\item \textbf{Fixed node set.} Only edges change; nodes are never added or
removed, and rewiring moves a single endpoint.
\item \textbf{Data-derived endpoints.} With real datasets, flow endpoints
are hashed from ports/services; the demand replay is a plausible
reconstruction, not a traced-topology ground truth.
\end{itemize}

%-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented P-LENS's formal problem statement and four interlocking
mechanisms---bounded plasticity debt as \emph{unresolved cost}, the
mutation budget as a constrained dual, interaction-aware joint selection,
and uncertainty-aware counterfactual replay for delayed credit---with a
honest pilot evaluation across nine policies and eight ablations, a
released runnable dataset/traffic/seed harness, and a stated negative
result about the ML forecaster's contributions at this scale. Its novelty
is an explicit mechanism composition: when, whether, and how much topology
change are answerable by auditable, measured quantities. The next stage
uses the already-implemented multi-scale study harness to establish
statistically powered comparative and ablation results.
